Distinct Post-Sentence Neural Patterns Representing Lexical Items vs. Sentence Integration

Authors: Yonghyeon Gwon, Chun Kee Chung

Overview

This study examines how the human brain retains linguistic information after the end of a sentence. It asks whether listeners preserve independent traces of lexical items as well as a holistic representation that integrates those items into sentence meaning.

Using the high-gamma envelope of electrocorticography (ECoG) signals from 15 patients with epilepsy, we distinguished neural activity encoding lexical content and negation from activity representing their sentence-level integration.

Research Question

Does the brain retain both individual lexical information and an integrated sentence representation after sentence comprehension, and where are these distinct representations encoded?

Approach

  • Recorded ECoG activity while participants performed an auditory question-and-answer task
  • Varied sentence content and negation to separate lexical-item information from sentence-specific integration
  • Fit label-based encoding models to high-gamma activity and quantified encoding strength across channels
  • Tracked whether lexical and integrated sentence information persisted after sentence offset and during response preparation

Output

Lexical information was represented across widespread cortical areas, whereas integrated sentence-specific information was strongly localized in the left posterior superior temporal gyrus. Both forms of information remained detectable while participants prepared their responses.

Published in NeuroImage, Volume 327, Article 121703 (2026).
